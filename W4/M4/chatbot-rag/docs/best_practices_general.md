# CV Best Practices — General Guidelines

## Length
- 1 page for candidates with under 3 years of experience
- 2 pages maximum for experienced professionals
- Never exceed 2 pages unless in academia or medicine
- Every line must earn its place — cut filler

## Structure (recommended order)
1. Contact information
2. Professional summary (3–4 lines max)
3. Work experience (reverse chronological)
4. Education
5. Skills
6. Certifications (if relevant)
7. Languages

## Contact Information
- Full name (large, at the top)
- Professional email address (firstname.lastname@domain.com)
- Phone number with country code
- LinkedIn URL (customized, not the default random string)
- City and country — full home address is unnecessary and outdated
- Do NOT include: photo, date of birth, marital status, nationality (unless required)

## Professional Summary
- 3–4 lines describing who you are, what you do, and what value you bring
- Avoid generic phrases: "hard worker", "team player", "passionate", "motivated"
- Should be tailored — not copy-pasted across every application
- Include years of experience, key domain, and one measurable achievement

## Work Experience
- Use reverse chronological order (most recent first)
- Include: job title, company name, location, dates (month/year)
- Use bullet points, not paragraphs
- Start every bullet with a strong action verb: Led, Built, Reduced, Increased, Designed
- Quantify wherever possible: "increased sales by 30%", "managed a team of 6"
- Focus on achievements, not just duties — what changed because you were there?
- Avoid: "Responsible for...", "Helped with...", "Assisted in..."
- Each role should have 3–5 bullets

## Education
- Degree, field, university name, graduation year
- Include GPA only if it is 3.5+ (or equivalent)
- Include relevant coursework only if you are a recent graduate with little experience
- Do not include high school if you have a university degree

## Skills Section
- Split into categories: Technical Skills, Tools, Languages, Soft Skills
- Do not list basic tools everyone has (Microsoft Word, email, internet)
- Be honest — if you cannot hold a conversation about it, do not list it
- Avoid rating yourself with stars or bars — it is subjective and meaningless

## Formatting
- Use a clean, professional font: Calibri, Arial, or Garamond (10–12pt)
- Consistent spacing, margins, and alignment throughout
- No tables, text boxes, or columns (ATS systems cannot read them)
- Save and send as PDF unless specifically asked for Word
- File name: FirstName_LastName_CV.pdf

## Common Mistakes
- Spelling and grammar errors — proofread at least twice
- Using "I" — CV is written in third person implied (no subject)
- Listing duties instead of achievements
- Including irrelevant jobs from 10+ years ago
- Generic objective statements ("seeking a challenging role...")
- Using the same CV for every application without tailoring
- Inconsistent date formats
- Too many fonts, colors, or design elements
